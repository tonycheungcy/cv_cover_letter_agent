# Cover Letter Playbook — Rules the Agent Enforces

## Cover Letter Structure (Section 4, generalized for engineering roles)

4 layers in this order:

1. **Hook** — why you, this role, this company
2. **Proof** — quantified evidence you can deliver
3. **Pattern** — cross-sector adaptability
4. **Practical** — logistics + forward-looking close

The CV proves *what you did*; the cover letter proves *why you are the right person for THIS job*.

---

## Layer 1 — The Hook

**Formula:**
```
I am writing to apply for the [EXACT JOB TITLE] at [COMPANY], a role that closely aligns with my experience in [CORE COMPETENCY 1], [CORE COMPETENCY 2], and [CORE COMPETENCY 3].
```

**Why it converts:**
- Mirrors the JD title precisely
- Names 3 core competencies from JD — proves you read it
- Positions you as a solution, not a supplicant

**Weak openings to avoid:**
- ❌ "I am excited to apply"
- ❌ "I am writing to express my interest"
- ❌ "I believe my skills would be a good fit"

**Real example:**
"I am writing to apply for the Senior Mechanical Design Engineer position, a role that closely aligns with my experience in product design, mechanical validation, and cross-functional collaboration."

---

## Layer 2 — The Proof

**Evidence ladder:** 2-3 paragraphs, each following:

```
[At Company], I [specific technical action with tool] that resulted in [metric]% [business outcome].
```

**Why it converts:**
- Every paragraph includes at least one number
- Real employer names serve as credibility anchors
- Distinguishes technical action from business outcome

**Real example:**
"At Dyson, I designed and validated a consumer product using CAD (SolidWorks) and FEA (ANSYS), reducing prototype iterations by 30% and cutting time-to-market by 3 weeks. I also led cross-functional reviews with manufacturing and quality teams to ensure design for manufacturability, improving first-pass yield by 15%."

**Student takeaway:** For every technical skill, ask "So what? What changed?" Then add a number.

---

## Layer 3 — The Pattern

**Formula:**
```
[Industry 1 metric] → [Industry 2 metric] → [Transferable capability]
```

**Why it converts:**
- Frames varied experience as evidence of learning speed
- Ends with a capability statement that echoes a universal business need

**Real example:**
"My cross-sector experience has strengthened my ability to translate engineering analysis into business outcomes. In a 6-month manufacturing internship, I reduced material waste by 12% through process optimization. In my capstone project, I designed a thermal management system that improved efficiency by 18%. Across contexts, rigorous engineering analysis drives measurable business results."

**Student takeaway:** Position your varied experiences as proof of adaptability — not as a scattered resume.

---

## Layer 4 — The Practical

**What to cover:**
- Location / office preferences
- Schedule availability
- Right-to-work status

**Why it converts:**
- Removes friction — recruiters hate logistical surprises
- Shows flexibility
- Ends forward-looking, not apologetic

**Real example:**
"I am available for the on-site schedule at the [City] facility and would welcome discussing how my background in mechanical design and product validation can contribute to [Company]'s continued success."

---

## JD Alignment Scorecard

Before sending, check: every JD requirement has evidence in your letter.

| JD Requirement | How Letter Addresses It | Evidence |
|:---|:---|:---|
| e.g., 3+ years product validation | Show internship + project experience | 6-month internship + capstone validation |
| CAD/CAM proficiency | Show specific tools used | SolidWorks, CATIA |
| Cross-functional collaboration | Show team projects | Led 3-person capstone team |

**If a requirement has no evidence:** either add it or acknowledge the gap — do not leave it blank.

---

## The 3 Rules That Apply to Both CV and Cover Letter

1. **Progressive scope**: each experience adds new skill/scope/responsibility
2. **Zero fluff**: every line passes the evidence test
3. **Quantified claims**: every technical claim gets a business answer
