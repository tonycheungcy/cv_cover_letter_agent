# CV Playbook — Rules the Agent Enforces

## CV Structure (Section 3, generalized for engineering roles)

4 sections in this order:

1. **Education & Credentials** — signals baseline competence
2. **About Me / Summary** — ATS keyword magnet + credibility anchor
3. **Experience** — where the argument lives (spend 70% of time here)
4. **Skills & Projects** — modern-stack signal + self-directed learning

---

## Rule 1 — Progressive Scope Signaling

Every experience adds a new **skill, scope, or responsibility**.

**3 dimensions to grow:**

| Dimension | What it means | Student mapping |
|-----------|--------------|-----------------|
| **Skill breadth** | New tool, new domain, new technique | Internship → CAD tools; Project → FEA/CFD |
| **Scope** | More components, more units, more users | 6-month internship → position in production facility |
| **Responsibility** | From executing to advising, from solo to leading | Project lead → team coordination |

**Example progression (any engineering discipline):**
- **Internship**: Executed routine CAD revisions under supervision
- **Capstone**: Led design of 3-component system, managed vendor coordination
- **Co-op**: Designed and validated products across 5 client accounts, reduced rework by 20%

---

## Rule 2 — Zero-Fluff Rule

**Delete these phrases immediately:**
- ❌ "Highly motivated professional"
- ❌ "Strong problem-solving abilities"
- ❌ "Passionate about engineering"
- ❌ "Seeking to leverage my skills in..."

**Replacement test:** For every adjective, ask "What's the evidence?"

**Before / After:**
- ❌ "Designed components using SolidWorks."
- ✅ "Designed 15+ mechanical components in SolidWorks, reducing design cycle by 25%."

---

## Rule 3 — Quantification Discipline

**4 types of numbers:**

| Type | Purpose | Example |
|------|---------|---------|
| **Percentages** | Change driven | +15%, -30%, +20% |
| **Absolute scale** | Scope owned | 50+ components, 5+ facilities |
| **Timeframes** | Urgency | Within 3 months project |
| **Cost / P&L** | Money impact | $50K savings |

**Honesty rule:** Never invent a metric. If you can't quantify, describe the decision you enabled.

---

## Bullet Formula

```
Technique → Scale → Business Outcome — Metric
```

**Components:**
- **Technique**: The method/tools you used (SolidWorks, ANSYS, Python, Lean)
- **Scale**: How many, how much, the scope (10+ designs, 500+ units)
- **Business Outcome**: What resulted (reduced time, improved quality, saved cost)
- **Metric**: The quantifiable result (+20% efficiency, -15% defects, $30K savings)

**Strong bullet example:**
"Optimized manufacturing process for 15 product lines using Lean Six Sigma, reducing cycle time by 15% and saving $45K annually."

---

## About Me Section

**3 jobs in 4-6 dense lines:**

1. **ATS keyword index** — tools/technologies from JD (SolidWorks, ANSYS, Python)
2. **Credibility anchor** — certifications, awards, publications (e.g., "Six Sigma Green Belt")
3. **Friction remover** — location, availability, right-to-work

**Example:**
"Tools: SolidWorks, CATIA, ANSYS Workbench, Python automation scripts. Certified Six Sigma Green Belt (ASQ). Right to work in US, Canada."

---

## Skills & Projects

**Two columns:**

**Skills (left):** Tools you can hit the ground running with
- CAD/CAM: SolidWorks, CATIA, Siemens NX
- Analysis: ANSYS, Abaqus, MATLAB
- Programming: Python, C++, MATLAB
- Manufacturing: Lean, GD&T, Six Sigma

**Projects (right):** Evidence of self-directed learning
- 2-3 max, each with ONE-line outcome

**Example project description:**
"Built lightweight UAV frame in SolidWorks — reduced weight by 22% while maintaining structural integrity."
